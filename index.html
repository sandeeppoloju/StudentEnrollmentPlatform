01 — Overview

Executive Summary

The market opportunity, the platform thesis, and how Activly wins a space no one has built correctly yet.

🎯
The Problem

Parents spend hours across Facebook Groups, school flyers, and disconnected websites searching for vetted after-school programs. Organizers have no unified, modern place to list and collect payment. No trusted, CORI-verified marketplace exists for youth enrichment in Massachusetts.

💡
The Solution

Activly is a Ticketmaster-style marketplace where families discover, enroll in, and pay for after-school and enrichment programs — with CORI-verified instructors, one-click COPPA-compliant enrollment, and a mobile-first experience that sets a new standard for the category.

🚀
The Opportunity

Massachusetts has roughly 900K children ages 5–17 1 and a large, fragmented enrichment-spending base Internal Est. 2. No dominant local marketplace exists. The closest competitor, Sawyer, starts around $129/mo with no CORI integration 3 — leaving a clear gap for a trust-first, MA-native platform.

✅
Platform thesis: Win Massachusetts deeply before expanding. Become the definitive marketplace for youth enrichment in New England — with hyper-local trust, exceptional UX, and CORI verification as a structural moat that competitors cannot quickly replicate. Commission revenue scales with enrollment volume while SaaS creates predictable ARR.

07 — Legal & Compliance

Legal & Compliance Review

Non-negotiable obligations across five areas. Budget $8,000–14,000 before launch. Engage a Massachusetts attorney for COPPA, CORI structure, and Terms of Service before the platform goes live.

⚠️
Priority action: Legal setup is the single most important pre-launch investment for a platform handling children's data and background checks. Cutting corners here creates existential liability. These are largely one-time costs with manageable annual review fees. This section reflects rules current as of 2025 but is not legal advice — confirm all specifics with licensed MA counsel.

👶 COPPA — Children's Online Privacy Protection Act

Applies to any platform that collects data from children under 13. As a youth enrollment marketplace, COPPA is the most critical compliance obligation. Note: the FTC's amended COPPA Rule took effect June 23, 2025, adding new obligations 8.

  • Parent or guardian must always be the account holder — never the child
  • Verifiable parental consent required before any child data is collected or stored
  • New (2025 Rule): separate verifiable parental consent required before sharing child data with third parties (e.g., for targeted advertising) 8
  • New (2025 Rule): a written data-retention policy is required; child data may not be kept longer than necessary for the purpose collected 8
  • Parents have the right to review, correct, and delete child data at any time — one-click in account settings
  • No behavioral advertising or retargeting using child data, ever
  • Data minimization: collect only name, age, and school grade — nothing beyond what is strictly necessary
  • No third-party tracking pixels on any pages that display child information
  • FTC enforcement ceiling: up to $53,088 per violation (2025 inflation-adjusted figure; rises annually) 9
💰 Attorney review: $2,000–4,000 | Termly policy automation: ~$350/yr | Review: annually + after any platform change

🔍 CORI — Massachusetts G.L. c. 6, §172

Every individual instructor or program contact must be checked individually. Entity EINs serve tax and payment purposes only — they do not substitute for individual CORI clearance.

  • Option A (Recommended): Checkr API integration ($29–35/check 4) — automated, full audit trail, no DCJIS certification required from Activly
  • Option B: Apply to MA DCJIS directly as a requestor — lower per-check cost after setup but significantly more administrative burden and principal-level checks required
  • Option C (Not recommended): Self-certification upload only — lowest friction but creates platform liability if an unchecked instructor causes harm
  • CORI clearances should be re-verified periodically (commonly every 1–3 years depending on role and organization policy) 10
  • Store only clearance status and date — never store raw CORI result documents in the database
  • Organizers cannot publish programs until all listed instructors hold valid CORI clearance
  • Build automated expiration alerts: notify organizers 30 days before any check expires
  • Display a CORI-verified badge prominently on all program listings
💰 Checkr: $29–35/check (charge organizers $39 — $5–10 margin) | MA employment-law attorney: $1,500–3,000 | Review: when MA law changes

🔒 Privacy Policy + Terms of Service

  • MA 201 CMR 17.00 requires a Written Information Security Program (WISP) for any company holding personal data of MA residents 11
  • Privacy Policy must disclose: data collected, how it is used, who it is shared with, how long it is retained, and how it can be deleted
  • Terms of Service must include: platform liability limitations, organizer certifications, payment terms, refund policy, account termination procedures, and a binding arbitration clause
  • Organizer certification: written acknowledgment that all instructors are CORI-checked, programs carry appropriate liability insurance, and facilities meet safety standards
  • Organizers must re-certify annually at subscription renewal
  • GDPR addendum required if any EU-based families use the platform
💰 Attorney drafting: $2,000–3,000 | Termly auto-policy: ~$350/yr | Review: annually

✍️ Waivers + Platform Insurance

  • Program-specific liability waivers: parent or guardian signs digitally at enrollment — timestamped and IP-logged
  • Waivers should be calibrated to activity type (contact sports carry different risk profiles than art classes)
  • Platform general liability insurance: $1,500–3,000/yr (Hiscox or Next Insurance are startup-accessible) Est.
  • Errors & Omissions and Cyber liability: $1,000–2,500/yr (Coalition specializes in tech startups) Est.
  • Require organizers to carry their own liability insurance as a condition of listing
💰 Platform insurance: $2,500–5,500/yr | Waiver templates: $500–1,000 attorney review | Review: annually

🏢 Business Formation + Intellectual Property

  • Form a Massachusetts LLC immediately — ~$500 filing fee, separates personal from business liability 12
  • Trademark search: use the USPTO's Trademark Search at tmsearch.uspto.gov (this replaced the retired TESS system in November 2023) 13, searching Classes 41 (education services) and 42 (software/technology) before finalizing the brand name
  • File trademark applications after the search clears — $350 per class base fee (2025 fee schedule), so ~$700 for both classes 14
  • Co-founder agreement: equity split, 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff, IP assignment to the company — execute on Day 1
  • Registered Agent service: $100–150/yr
💰 LLC: ~$500 | Trademark: ~$700 (2 classes, base fee) | Co-founder agreement: $1,000–2,000
📋
Total legal budget at launch: $8,000–14,000 one-time + $3,000–6,000/yr ongoing (insurance, policy maintenance, annual attorney review). Treat this as infrastructure — not overhead.
